# Interview guide

Use this guide when the author is developing a new article or when a draft lacks concrete material.

## Conversation rhythm

Ask one to three questions per turn. Select the questions that can most change or deepen the article, then follow the author's answer. Do not present a comprehensive intake form.

When an answer remains abstract, ask for an observable instance: where it happened, who was present, what they did or said, what came immediately before and after, and why the author remembers it. Seek details that carry the argument rather than decorative sensory description.

Keep a working distinction among:

- events the author witnessed or participated in;
- events reported by someone else;
- what the author felt at the time;
- what the author believes now;
- explanations that remain hypotheses;
- examples that challenge the emerging argument.

Do not display this as a ledger unless it would help the author resolve confusion.

## Angles for follow-up

Choose among these based on the conversation:

- Scene: What single incident would let a reader watch the problem occur?
- Change: When did this begin, and what was different before?
- Incentives: What did each participant want, fear, or need to demonstrate?
- Mechanism: Which rule, metric, reporting practice, or power relationship shaped the behavior?
- Cost: Who performed the hidden work or absorbed the delayed consequence?
- Benefit: Who gained from the arrangement, and how?
- Boundary: When would the same behavior be necessary or useful?
- Alternative explanation: What else could explain the same events?
- Author's position: How did the author participate, benefit, comply, misread the situation, or change his mind?
- Native language: What terms did people actually use, and what did those terms conceal or reveal?
- Reader: What should become visible to the reader that was previously easy to miss?

## Productive challenge

Do not merely affirm the author's first interpretation. Point out weak evidence, compressed causal chains, selective standards, and claims broader than the examples support. Ask for a counterexample that could genuinely modify the thesis, not a ceremonial concession.

The goal is a more exact position, not a balanced list of views. Stop pursuing a line of questioning when it no longer changes the article.
